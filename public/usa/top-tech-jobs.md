## Role-by-role breakdown for United States

### Frontend Developer in United States

Demand for frontend developers in United States is rated very high with +16% YoY growth in posted openings. A junior typically starts around $65,000, mid-level engineers with three to five years of shipped work land near $105,000, and seniors who own architecture or lead a team reach $160,000. Most of these roles concentrate in San Francisco Bay Area, Seattle and New York, though remote and hybrid contracts have widened the pool considerably since 2024.

The hiring signal employers look for is depth, not breadth. For this role that means demonstrable strength in React / Next.js, TypeScript and Tailwind CSS, plus one production system you can talk through end to end — how it was designed, what broke, and what you changed afterwards. - Where the openings are: Google, Microsoft, Amazon and Apple plus global employers hiring locally.

## Which role should you pick?

Pick on three axes: what you can sustain learning for twelve months, what the United States market is actually hiring for, and what pays enough to justify the switch. If you enjoy visible output, frontend and mobile fit. If you enjoy systems and correctness, backend, cloud and DevOps fit. If you enjoy analysis and ambiguity, data and AI roles fit. ## Seven mistakes that cost candidates offers

1. Learning five languages shallowly instead of one deeply. Depth in one stack beats a tour of many every single time.
2. Tutorial loops. If you have not built something unplanned and slightly beyond your ability this month, you are not progressing.
3. A CV that lists technologies but no outcomes. Every bullet should contain a verb and a number.
4. Applying only through job boards. Referrals, community events in San Francisco Bay Area and direct outreach to hiring managers convert far better.
5. Skipping fundamentals. Interviews test data structures, complexity and system design no matter how modern the stack is.
6. No public work. A single well-documented repository or write-up outperforms ten private practice projects.
7. Treating rejection as verdict rather than data. Ask for feedback, fix one thing, and go again.

### What is the easiest tech job to get in United States?

QA automation, technical support engineering and data analysis have the lowest entry barrier, because employers accept demonstrated tooling skill in place of years of experience. All three are also legitimate on-ramps into engineering within eighteen months.

### Are there entry-level tech jobs in United States in 2027?

Yes, but they are concentrated in San Francisco Bay Area, Seattle and New York and in companies that run structured graduate programmes. Applying within the first week of a posting materially improves response rates.

### Do I need to speak English to work in tech in United States?

For international product companies, English is usually sufficient. For local enterprises, public sector and customer-facing roles, working proficiency in English is often required or heavily preferred.

## Cost of living, tax and what the numbers actually mean

Every figure on this page is annual gross in USD. Take-home pay depends on United States's income tax bands, social contributions and any employer-side benefits, so two identical gross offers can differ meaningfully in what reaches your account. Before comparing an offer against another country, convert both to purchasing power rather than exchange rate: a lower headline number in a cheaper city frequently leaves more disposable income than a headline number in San Francisco Bay Area.

- **Housing** is the largest single variable between San Francisco Bay Area, Seattle, New York, Austin and Boston and smaller cities, and it moves effective pay more than a one-level promotion does.
- **Employer type** matters as much as title — funded product companies, global captives and local services firms pay noticeably different bands for the same work.
- **Equity and bonus** are worth discounting heavily unless the company is public or you understand the terms in detail.
- **Remote arbitrage** works both ways: employers increasingly band remote pay by location, so confirm the policy before assuming a hub salary from outside the hub.

## Working internationally from United States

Two routes exist and they are very different. Relocation means employer sponsorship, a visa process measured in months, and a compensation reset to the destination market's band. Remote employment means keeping your UTC-5 to UTC-8 base while being paid against another market's band, usually through a contractor arrangement or an employer-of-record. The remote route is faster and reversible; the relocation route pays more and takes longer. Whichever you pursue, English written communication and a public body of work do more to open the door than any certificate — hiring managers abroad cannot verify your degree easily, but they can read your repositories.

## Frequently asked questions about tech jobs in United States

### How many applications does a first tech job in United States take?

Realistically 40 to 120 tailored applications over eight to sixteen weeks, with referrals converting several times better than cold applications. Untailored volume applying produces worse results than half the applications with a matched CV.

### Are there entry-level roles at all right now?

Yes, but they are concentrated in services companies, scale-ups and internal platform teams rather than in high-profile product companies. Local employers such as Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Apple, Meta and Nvidia hire in cohorts — apply when their cycles open rather than continuously.

### How reliable are the United States figures on this page?

They are 2027 market-band estimates built from public compensation datasets and job postings, expressed as annual gross in USD before tax. Use them as a negotiating anchor, not a guarantee — company type and role scope move a band more than the job title does. Cross-check your own number with the free [salary predictor](https://www.skillta.tech/salary-predictor).

### Do I need to live in San Francisco Bay Area and Seattle to get hired?

No, but it still helps. Hiring density is highest in San Francisco Bay Area, Seattle, New York, Austin and Boston, and hybrid roles there usually pay above the national band. Fully remote roles have narrowed that gap, and your UTC-5 to UTC-8 overlap with the employer's core hours matters more than your postcode for distributed teams.
