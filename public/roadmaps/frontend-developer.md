## What a Frontend Developer actually does day to day

Frontend developers bring designs to life. You'll build the visual and interactive parts of websites and apps that users directly interact with. In practice the week looks less like continuous coding and more like a mix of building, reviewing, debugging and deciding. A typical day includes a short stand-up, two to four hours of focused build time, code review for teammates, and at least one conversation about scope or trade-offs. The people who progress fastest in this role are the ones who treat those conversations as part of the job rather than as an interruption to it.

- **Morning:** triage anything that broke overnight, then take the highest-leverage task rather than the easiest one.
- **Core hours:** deep work on the current increment — HTML/CSS, JavaScript and React/Vue/Angular are the tools you will touch most.
- **Reviews:** reading other people's changes is the fastest way to learn a codebase and the fastest way to build trust.
- **Documentation:** a short written note about why a decision was made saves hours for the next person, often you in three months.
- **Learning:** the field moves; an hour a week on fundamentals beats a weekend binge every quarter.

This path suits you if several of the following are true. It is worth being honest here — switching after six months costs far more than choosing carefully now.

- You have a strong eye for design and aesthetics

- You're creative and detail-oriented

## Frontend Developer interview preparation

Interview loops for this role typically run four to six stages. Expect a recruiter screen, a technical screen on fundamentals, a practical exercise or take-home, a deep-dive on your own projects, and a hiring-manager conversation about ownership and collaboration.

| Round | What is tested | Preparation that works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Screening | Motivation, communication, salary alignment | A 90-second summary of your work and a researched range |
| Technical fundamentals | HTML/CSS, JavaScript and React/Vue/Angular | Daily reps for four weeks, explained out loud |
| Practical exercise | Code quality, tests, judgement about scope | Timebox it and document what you deliberately left out |
| Project deep-dive | Whether you actually built what your CV claims | Be able to justify every architectural choice you made |
| Hiring manager | Ownership, conflict, how you handle being wrong | Six STAR stories including one genuine failure |

- **TypeScript:** explain how you would debug a problem involving typescript in production.
- **Responsive Design:** explain how you would debug a problem involving responsive design in production.
- **Git:** walk through a trade-off you made using git and what you would do differently.
- **UI/UX Principles:** walk through a trade-off you made using ui/ux principles and what you would do differently.
- **HTML/CSS:** describe how html/css fits into the systems you have built.
- **JavaScript:** compare two approaches within javascript and justify your default choice.
- **React/Vue/Angular:** describe how react/vue/angular fits into the systems you have built.

## Mistakes that slow people down

1. Collecting tutorials instead of finishing projects. Completion is the skill being trained.
2. Learning adjacent tools before the core ones. Get HTML/CSS and JavaScript solid first.
3. Building only what the tutorial shows. The learning happens when something breaks and nobody has written the fix down.
4. Waiting until you feel ready to apply. Interview practice is a skill and it is trained by interviewing.
5. No public trail. A deployed link and a written case study is worth more than a private repository.
6. Ignoring fundamentals because the stack is modern. Complexity, data modelling and debugging are still what interviews test.

## Frontend Developer — frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to become a frontend developer?

6-12 months to job-ready for someone starting from scratch and studying 10–15 hours a week. People coming from an adjacent technical role usually move faster because they already understand how teams ship software.

### Is Frontend Developer a good career in 2027?

Demand is rated very high. Excellent — every company needs frontend developers. Growing demand for React/Next.js specialists.

### Do I need a degree to become a frontend developer?

No, though it still helps for visa-sponsored roles and large enterprises. What replaces it is evidence: deployed projects, a public code history, and the ability to explain your decisions clearly.

### How hard is it really?

Difficulty is moderate — roughly 3 out of 10. Frontend changes fast. You'll constantly learn new frameworks and tools. CSS can be frustrating. But the creative satisfaction is unmatched.

### What should I learn first?

Start with Fundamentals — specifically HTML5, CSS3 and JavaScript Basics. Everything later in the roadmap assumes this foundation.

### Can I switch to Frontend Developer from a non-technical background?

Yes, and thousands do each year. The realistic timeline is 6-12 months (basics) → 2-3 years (expert), the main risk is quitting in month four, and the strongest mitigation is a public build streak plus one person who expects progress from you weekly.

### Will AI replace frontend developers?

AI has changed the work rather than removed it. Code generation raised the floor, and the value moved toward design, debugging, evaluating correctness and understanding systems — the parts current models handle least reliably.
