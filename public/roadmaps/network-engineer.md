## What a Network Engineer actually does day to day

Network engineers design, implement, and manage computer networks. You ensure that data flows reliably and securely across organizations. In practice the week looks less like continuous coding and more like a mix of building, reviewing, debugging and deciding. A typical day includes a short stand-up, two to four hours of focused build time, code review for teammates, and at least one conversation about scope or trade-offs. The people who progress fastest in this role are the ones who treat those conversations as part of the job rather than as an interruption to it.

- **Morning:** triage anything that broke overnight, then take the highest-leverage task rather than the easiest one.
- **Core hours:** deep work on the current increment — TCP/IP, Routing & Switching and Firewalls are the tools you will touch most.
- **Reviews:** reading other people's changes is the fastest way to learn a codebase and the fastest way to build trust.
- **Documentation:** a short written note about why a decision was made saves hours for the next person, often you in three months.
- **Learning:** the field moves; an hour a week on fundamentals beats a weekend binge every quarter.

## Network Engineer interview preparation

Interview loops for this role typically run four to six stages. Expect a recruiter screen, a technical screen on fundamentals, a practical exercise or take-home, a deep-dive on your own projects, and a hiring-manager conversation about ownership and collaboration.

| Round | What is tested | Preparation that works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Screening | Motivation, communication, salary alignment | A 90-second summary of your work and a researched range |
| Technical fundamentals | TCP/IP, Routing & Switching and Firewalls | Daily reps for four weeks, explained out loud |
| Practical exercise | Code quality, tests, judgement about scope | Timebox it and document what you deliberately left out |
| Project deep-dive | Whether you actually built what your CV claims | Be able to justify every architectural choice you made |
| Hiring manager | Ownership, conflict, how you handle being wrong | Six STAR stories including one genuine failure |

- **Automation:** compare two approaches within automation and justify your default choice.
- **Security:** compare two approaches within security and justify your default choice.
- **TCP/IP:** walk through a trade-off you made using tcp/ip and what you would do differently.
- **Routing & Switching:** compare two approaches within routing & switching and justify your default choice.
- **Firewalls:** compare two approaches within firewalls and justify your default choice.
- **CCNA/CCNP:** walk through a trade-off you made using ccna/ccnp and what you would do differently.
- **Cloud Networking:** compare two approaches within cloud networking and justify your default choice.

## Mistakes that slow people down

1. Collecting tutorials instead of finishing projects. Completion is the skill being trained.
2. Learning adjacent tools before the core ones. Get TCP/IP and Routing & Switching solid first.
3. Building only what the tutorial shows. The learning happens when something breaks and nobody has written the fix down.
4. Waiting until you feel ready to apply. Interview practice is a skill and it is trained by interviewing.
5. No public trail. A deployed link and a written case study is worth more than a private repository.
6. Ignoring fundamentals because the stack is modern. Complexity, data modelling and debugging are still what interviews test.

## Network Engineer — frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to become a network engineer?

10-16 months to job-ready for someone starting from scratch and studying 15–20 hours a week. People coming from an adjacent technical role usually move faster because they already understand how teams ship software.

### Is Network Engineer a good career in 2027?

Demand is rated high. Strong — networks are foundational. Cloud networking and SD-WAN are growing rapidly.

### Do I need a degree to become a network engineer?

No, though it still helps for visa-sponsored roles and large enterprises. What replaces it is evidence: deployed projects, a public code history, and the ability to explain your decisions clearly.

### How hard is it really?

Difficulty is hard — roughly 4 out of 10. Network outages are high-pressure events. On-call is standard. Physical infrastructure work can mean crawling under desks. But networking skills are universally needed.

### What should I learn first?

Start with Fundamentals — specifically Networking Basics, Routing & Switching and Lab Setup. Everything later in the roadmap assumes this foundation.

### Can I switch to Network Engineer from a non-technical background?

Yes, and thousands do each year. The realistic timeline is 10-16 months (entry) → 4-6 years (expert), the main risk is quitting in month four, and the strongest mitigation is a public build streak plus one person who expects progress from you weekly.

### Will AI replace network engineers?

AI has changed the work rather than removed it. Code generation raised the floor, and the value moved toward design, debugging, evaluating correctness and understanding systems — the parts current models handle least reliably.
