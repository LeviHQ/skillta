## What a Developer Advocate actually does day to day

Developer advocates (DevRel) are the voice of developers within a company and the voice of the company to developers. You create content, build community, and influence product direction. In practice the week looks less like continuous coding and more like a mix of building, reviewing, debugging and deciding. A typical day includes a short stand-up, two to four hours of focused build time, code review for teammates, and at least one conversation about scope or trade-offs. The people who progress fastest in this role are the ones who treat those conversations as part of the job rather than as an interruption to it.

- **Morning:** triage anything that broke overnight, then take the highest-leverage task rather than the easiest one.
- **Core hours:** deep work on the current increment — Technical Skills, Content Creation and Public Speaking are the tools you will touch most.
- **Reviews:** reading other people's changes is the fastest way to learn a codebase and the fastest way to build trust.
- **Documentation:** a short written note about why a decision was made saves hours for the next person, often you in three months.
- **Learning:** the field moves; an hour a week on fundamentals beats a weekend binge every quarter.

## Developer Advocate interview preparation

Interview loops for this role typically run four to six stages. Expect a recruiter screen, a technical screen on fundamentals, a practical exercise or take-home, a deep-dive on your own projects, and a hiring-manager conversation about ownership and collaboration.

| Round | What is tested | Preparation that works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Screening | Motivation, communication, salary alignment | A 90-second summary of your work and a researched range |
| Technical fundamentals | Technical Skills, Content Creation and Public Speaking | Daily reps for four weeks, explained out loud |
| Practical exercise | Code quality, tests, judgement about scope | Timebox it and document what you deliberately left out |
| Project deep-dive | Whether you actually built what your CV claims | Be able to justify every architectural choice you made |
| Hiring manager | Ownership, conflict, how you handle being wrong | Six STAR stories including one genuine failure |

- **Product Feedback:** explain how you would debug a problem involving product feedback in production.
- **Technical Skills:** explain how you would debug a problem involving technical skills in production.
- **Content Creation:** describe how content creation fits into the systems you have built.
- **Public Speaking:** explain how you would debug a problem involving public speaking in production.
- **Community Building:** compare two approaches within community building and justify your default choice.
- **Technical Writing:** describe how technical writing fits into the systems you have built.
- **Social Media:** walk through a trade-off you made using social media and what you would do differently.

## Mistakes that slow people down

1. Collecting tutorials instead of finishing projects. Completion is the skill being trained.
2. Learning adjacent tools before the core ones. Get Technical Skills and Content Creation solid first.
3. Building only what the tutorial shows. The learning happens when something breaks and nobody has written the fix down.
4. Waiting until you feel ready to apply. Interview practice is a skill and it is trained by interviewing.
5. No public trail. A deployed link and a written case study is worth more than a private repository.
6. Ignoring fundamentals because the stack is modern. Complexity, data modelling and debugging are still what interviews test.

## Developer Advocate — frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to become a developer advocate?

Variable — requires prior dev experience for someone starting from scratch and studying 10–15 hours a week. People coming from an adjacent technical role usually move faster because they already understand how teams ship software.

### Is Developer Advocate a good career in 2027?

Demand is rated high. Strong — every developer-focused company needs DevRel. The role is becoming essential for developer-first products.

### Do I need a degree to become a developer advocate?

No, though it still helps for visa-sponsored roles and large enterprises. What replaces it is evidence: deployed projects, a public code history, and the ability to explain your decisions clearly.

### How hard is it really?

Difficulty is moderate — roughly 3 out of 10. It's not all fun conference talks. You'll spend a lot of time on planes, in airports, and recovering from jet lag. Measuring DevRel ROI is always challenging. But if you love teaching and community, it's the best job in tech.

### What should I learn first?

Start with Technical Foundation — specifically Deep Technical Skills, Multiple Languages and Product Knowledge. Everything later in the roadmap assumes this foundation.

### Can I switch to Developer Advocate from a non-technical background?

Yes, and thousands do each year. The realistic timeline is Requires 2-4 years dev experience first, the main risk is quitting in month four, and the strongest mitigation is a public build streak plus one person who expects progress from you weekly.

### Will AI replace developer advocates?

AI has changed the work rather than removed it. Code generation raised the floor, and the value moved toward design, debugging, evaluating correctness and understanding systems — the parts current models handle least reliably.
